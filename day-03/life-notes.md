### Your mom may be reacting to something different than you are

I wouldn't assume she thinks you're lazy unless she's actually said that.

You may be thinking:

> "I'm building something that could eventually impact thousands of people."

She may simply be thinking:

> "My son doesn't currently have predictable income and needs to be able to support himself."

Those aren't necessarily opposing beliefs.

And because she watched your great pops operate a business with an actual contract producing consistent money, she may understandably associate a "real business" with money reliably coming through the door.

So instead of trying to convince your family of the **vision**, show them the **plan**.

For example:

**October–December 2026**

Job target: 10–15 strong applications/networking conversations each week.

Business target: 50 Oklahoma schools/nonprofits/workforce organizations contacted each week.

Sales target: 5 discovery calls per week.

Revenue target: land 1–2 paid workshops/contracts.

FDE target: continue your daily technical curriculum and portfolio.

Personal target: determine the monthly income you need to move out and build a savings target around it.

Now your answer when someone asks, "What's your plan?" isn't:

*"I'm trying to make Between Two Divs work."*

It's:

*"I'm pursuing technical roles for stable income while building a pipeline of school and workforce-development contracts. I'm giving myself measurable targets every week and reviewing them every Sunday."*

That's considerably different.

### And I wouldn't ignore what you said about music.

There's a pattern worth noticing—not as a judgment, but because you've experienced it twice.

Your family had a business opportunity → everyone got burned out → you walked away → later you recognized what it could have become.

The lesson I'd put in your second brain isn't **"I should have kept the cleaning company"** or even **"I shouldn't have stopped music."**

> **Don't abandon something that is showing real evidence of working simply because the people closest to me don't understand where it could go. Instead, evaluate it by evidence: customers, revenue, demand, growth, and whether I still want to do the work.**

At the same time, don't keep something alive solely because you believe in it. **Vision needs evidence.**

That balance is important.
